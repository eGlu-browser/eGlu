# Changelog

What changes in Safience from one version to the next, newest first. A change that fixes or adds something adds its line under **Unreleased** the day it is merged.

## Unreleased

### Added

- In the iPhone's desktop view, the cursor pushed against the top or the bottom of the page scrolls it, up at the top and down at the bottom: the view follows the cursor only as far as the desktop goes, and what the finger moves past that, the page scrolls by, further the faster, as the cursor goes (`DesktopView.moveCursor` returns the push). A flick that set out with the cursor already at that end glides on, as two fingers' does; one that only got there stops with the finger, so a cursor sent down to a button at the bottom doesn't send the page off. A drag with the button held scrolls the same way, as a selection dragged to a window's edge does. The wheel events go to what is just inside the page under the cursor: on the desktop's last row nothing is under it, and a scroller there never heard of a scroll.
- On iPhone, a swipe sideways on the bar goes to the tab beside, as in Safari: the page and the address go with the finger, the next tab's coming in beside them, and letting go carries on at the finger's speed to whichever it was heading for. At the first and the last tab the drag gives less and less and springs back; it never goes round.
- A link has a menu, as Safari's has, at a long press on iPhone and iPad and at a secondary click on iPad: Open in New Tab, Open in Background, Copy Link and Share…, under the link's address; a tap on the lifted link goes there. Never over a menu of the page's own: after a secondary click the bridge says whether the page kept the contextmenu event to itself, and the menu shows only if it didn't (no answer in time counts as kept); a page whose style turns callouts off gets none; a site that keeps its own menus gets none (below); and a hands-off page gets none. The app no longer turns callouts off on every page (`base.css`), which is what kept WebKit from asking for a link's menu at all.
- Sites that keep their own menus: web apps that draw a menu of their own at a secondary click or a long press get none of the app's or WebKit's over it. 49 ship in the list (`OwnMenus.swift`), by the host the app itself runs on: Figma, Canva, Miro, Google Docs, Drive and Gmail, Outlook, Notion, Linear, Jira, Slack, Discord, VS Code for the Web and others, `docs.google.com` and not `google.com`, so a search's results keep their link menu. Settings › Link menu › Sites with their own menus turns any of them off and adds sites of one's own, by address; the page's menu, a tab's menu and the palette have Use This Site's Own Menus and Show Link Menu on This Site (⌃⌥⇧L) for the site on screen. Only what differs from the list is kept.
- A picture has WebKit's own menu at a long press, as in Safari: Share…, Save to Photos, Copy, Copy Subject and Look Up. A picture that is a link has the link's menu with Save to Photos under it. Saving asks to add to Photos, never to read them (`NSPhotoLibraryAddUsageDescription`).
- Back and forward by a swipe. On iPhone and on mobile sites it is Safari's: a finger from the left or right edge, the page before sliding in. On a desktop page WebKit's own swipe stays off, since with the wheel bridged it would take two fingers sideways from a canvas that pans with them; the app's own does it instead (`BackSwipe.swift`, `TrackpadSwipe`): a finger dragged in from the page's edge, or two fingers sideways on the trackpad, brings an arrow in from that edge, which fills once letting go would take the step. The trackpad's swipe is the page's first: it goes back only when the swipe set out sideways and the page left every wheel event of it unused (`bridge.js` answers `free`), which a page that pans or scrolls sideways there, or sets `overscroll-behavior-x`, never does; an answer that doesn't come counts as used. A hands-off page and a PDF, which WebKit scrolls itself, get WebKit's swipe.
- Pull down to load the page again, on iPhone and on mobile sites, where a finger scrolls the page. Not in Desktop View and not on a desktop page.
- Find on Page (⌃⌥F, the page's menu, a tab's menu, the palette): WebKit's own find bar, with the count and next and previous. ⌘F stays the page's: the find bar's keys are WebKit's only while the bar shows.
- A welcome on the first launch, over the start page (`Welcome.swift`): what Safience is, and that it has no account to make; then a first space to name, colour and give an icon, bookmarks to bring from Chrome, Firefox or Safari, and the default browser and iCloud. Every step can be skipped, the space's colour is the welcome's own as it is chosen, and whoever already has a workspace never sees it.
- Suggestions under the address as it is typed, as in Safari and Chrome: the space's open tabs and pinned tabs, its bookmarks, the browser's commands and the search engine's suggestions, each row naming where it came from (Open Tab, Pinned Tab, Bookmark, Command, or the engine's name) beside the phrase it completes, the letters typed plain and the rest in bold. A page is offered by its address when the letters start it and by its title otherwise, and the letters have to start a word, so "fig" finds figma.com but never Configure. The page the letters start comes first, then the engine's suggestions, then the rest; one row a page, an open tab before its bookmark; commands need two letters and take two rows at most. ↑ and ↓ choose a row and Return takes it, an open tab by going to it, and a tap or a click does too; with no row chosen, Return sends what was typed, as before. Under the field on iPad, in both layouts, and over the bar on iPhone, scrolling when the keyboard leaves little room. The engine's suggestions come from Google, DuckDuckGo or Bing, whichever Settings › Search names, a moment after the typing pauses, without cookies; an address typed is never sent. Settings › Search › Search engine suggestions turns them off (`Autocomplete.swift`, `Suggestions.swift`).
- Apple Intelligence in the address bar, on iOS and iPadOS 26 and later where it is on: once typing pauses, Apple's on-device model reads the words with the space's tabs, pinned tabs, bookmarks and the browser's commands, and finds up to three by what the words mean rather than their letters ("put this on my home screen" finds Share Page, "탭 고정" finds Pin or Unpin Tab, "the onboarding file" a Figma tab of that name). They show after the page the letters start, still naming where they came from, with sparkles beside it. Where the engine's suggestions aren't asked for (turned off, or an engine without them), the model suggests up to three searches instead, marked Apple Intelligence. Nothing leaves the device for it; fewer than three letters and addresses are left to the letters alone. Settings › Search › Suggest with Apple Intelligence turns it off. FoundationModels is linked weakly, so iOS 17 and 18 start without it (`Intelligence.swift`).
- Settings › App icon: seven more icons beside Safience, the app's own, each the globe and pointer drawn its own way: Engineer as a Mac window filling the icon, its title bar with the three buttons along the top and on its screen the globe and a 1-bit I-beam with sharp, fanned ends, on one grid of phosphor pixels, Diazo as an atlas engraving in graphite on warm white, with hatching, under a pen-tool cursor shaped as Figma's in Liquid Glass, a black nib with white lines on the paper and Figma's white nib with black lines in dark mode, Signal as the globe's grid and the arrow lit on one LED board, Lilac as build 9's ring made a candy sticker, a lilac ring and a pink oval laid over it each its own piece of frosted, see-through Liquid Glass, the oval blurring the ring where it crosses it, on a frosted white disc over a lilac-to-peach gradient, Oxide in the style of macOS's leather years, oxblood leather with a teal leather patch stitched on in cream, the globe stamped in gold foil, black leather in dark mode, and a cursor made as an enamel pin, black glossy enamel in a polished gold rim, Citrus as a pop poster with Ben-Day dots, Midnight as a star chart. Each shows in light or dark as the screen does, on the system's dark ground in dark mode except Oxide's black leather and Midnight's navy, and the system keeps the choice.
- ⌘-click opens a link in a new tab, as in Safari: behind the tab on screen, which stays where it was, and after the tabs ⌘-clicked from it before, so they keep their order. ⌘⇧-click opens it in front, and a mouse's middle button behind. A button that only goes to a page (a form that asks with GET, a button whose script goes somewhere) does the same; a form that sends something (POST), back and forward, and a ⌘-click a page keeps for itself (Figma's, which picks a layer) stay as they were (`NewTabClick.swift`).
- Ads and trackers blocked, by WebKit's own content blocker, before they load: EasyList and EasyPrivacy (by the EasyList authors, under CC BY-SA) ship with the app, turned on the device into WebKit's rules (`ContentBlocking.swift`, which leaves out what WebKit can't say exactly rather than saying it loosely) and compiled into rule lists kept until the lists change; newer lists come from easylist.to when they say they have expired, four days at the soonest, and only if they changed. Every page gets them but a hands-off one, Google's sign-in among them. A site's menu has Allow Ads on This Site and Block Ads on This Site (⌃⌥⇧A, or the palette), kept for the site; Settings › Ads and trackers turns it off everywhere and says how many rules there are and how new the lists are; Diagnostics' last line says whether this page has them. A filter blocks what a page loads, never the page itself. Scriptlets and procedural hiding, which need a script in the page, aren't done.
- On iPhone, the pinned tabs are a dock at the bottom of the tab overview, as the iPhone's apps are: their sites' icons on a capsule of glass only as wide as they need, floating over the tabs as they scroll, the grid's last row clearing it; the overview is a light grey tray under white cards, as Safari's is, so the glass shows against it; the icons scroll sideways when there are more than fit, the one on screen ringed in the space's colour as its card is; a long press has the tab's menu.
- The iPhone's desktop view shows hover and cursor styles: the page's :hover styles follow the cursor (copied by bridge.js onto an attribute the elements under the cursor get, media queries for a pointer that hovers answered yes; a style sheet from another origin can't be read, so its hover styles stay off), and the cursor takes the shape CSS names, a hand over a link, the text beam, arrows to resize, not-allowed and the rest.
- Settings on iPhone › Desktop View › Cursor speed: from half to two and a half times the usual.
- On iPhone, the tab overview as Safari's: a card swiped sideways is thrown off and closed, with a firm tap where letting go will close it; a long press on a card has Copy Link, Share, Pin Tab, Move to Space, Arrange Tabs By Title or Website (pinned tabs stay first, a split's two tabs together), Close Other Tabs, Close All Tabs and Close Tab; a long press on the bar's tabs button has New Tab, Close This Tab and Close All Tabs. Close All keeps pinned tabs. The overview is UIKit now (TabOverview.swift), so a sideways swipe on a card and a scroll of the grid never fight over the touch.
- Bookmark folders on the start page: New Folder (the folder button by the heading makes one where you are), and Rename… and Move to… on a bookmark's or a folder's menu, Move to… listing every folder by its path and the top level from inside one. A folder never goes into itself. Folders sync and import as before.
- Desktop view on iPhone (the page's menu): the page laid out at an iPad Pro 13-inch's size (1376 by 1032), and the screen a trackpad for it, as in Jump Desktop's trackpad mode. One finger moves a cursor, further the faster it goes, and the view follows it near an edge; a tap clicks and two quick taps double-click; touch and hold, then move, drags; two fingers tap for the right button and move to scroll, gliding on after a flick; a pinch zooms the view. The page's own cursor shows where it has one. A see-through minimap at the top right shows the whole page, in colour where it shows and in black and white elsewhere; a tap on it goes there, and a drag throws it to another corner, as picture in picture does. The keyboard button types into what was last clicked, with Escape, Tab and the arrows over the keyboard, and a Korean syllable reaches the page whole. Clicks, scrolls and typing reach the page as script events from bridge.js, which a few sites ignore; it is never on a hands-off page, and leaving it loads the page again as the window's size has it.
- iPhone, and the iPhone Duo folded and unfolded. A phone-width window has its bar at the bottom, as in Safari and Comet: the address across the width with the page's menu beside it, and under it back, forward, the space in the middle, a new tab and the tabs, every control 44 points. The bar floats over the page, which runs to the bottom of the screen and shows through it, blurred more the lower it goes; under Reduce Transparency it is solid. While an address is typed, only the address and Cancel ride above the keyboard. The address shows the page's title instead when Settings › The tab you are on shows says so, as the iPad's tab does (sign-in pages always show where). The Tabs button, or a swipe up on the bar, shows the space's tabs as pictures of their pages, with New Tab and Done at the bottom: the bar turns into a sheet of them, its top edge under the finger, with a light tap once letting go would open it and a soft one as it lands; a flick carries on at the finger's speed, and a swipe down, Done or a tab takes it back into the bar. Phone-width windows get mobile sites, with Safari's iPhone user agent, touch scrolling and pinch zoom; windows at least 600 by 500 points get desktop sites. Request Desktop or Mobile Site (⌃⌥⇧M, or the command palette) changes it for a site and remembers. On a phone the command palette has Cancel beside its field.
- Spaces, bookmarks and tabs in iCloud, off until Settings › iCloud turns it on, through CloudKit's private database and `CKSyncEngine`, as Apple has apps sync: each space's name, colour, icon, bookmarks and pinned tabs show on every iPhone and iPad signed in to the same account, a record per bookmark so two devices' edits both stay; each device's open tabs show on the others, in the tab overview and the palette, to pick up from, sign-in pages and addresses carrying a credential left out. Sign-ins, cookies and site data stay on each device. Turning it on joins spaces with the same name and keeps both sets of bookmarks; what earlier builds kept in iCloud's key-value store comes over once.
- Safience Sync for Chrome (`Extension/`): a Chrome profile pairs with a space through the same iCloud records, signed in with the Apple Account, with no server in between. Its bookmarks bar syncs both ways with the space's bookmarks, pinned tabs both ways (never closing a tab), and each side's open tabs show on the other. Chrome, Edge, Brave and other Chromium browsers.
- Signed with Apple's browser entitlement, which Apple granted: iPadOS offers Safience as the default browser, pages can use passkeys and service workers, and Share Page (⌃⌥S, or the tab's menu) puts Add to Home Screen in the share sheet. Settings says whether Safience is the default browser and opens Default Apps to make it so. A team without the entitlement turns it off in `Config/Local.xcconfig`.
- A new app icon, made with Icon Composer: a globe drawn as a technical drawing, bright white lines on an ink disc, sharp where the globe faces the viewer and the light, softening and fading as the sphere turns away toward its lower right edge, with a bright blue degree scale and centre lines, and a bright blue cursor over it, in Liquid Glass on drafting paper, on iOS and iPadOS 26 and later, with its dark, tinted and clear looks, and a flat picture Xcode draws from it for earlier versions. The Chrome extension's icons are drawn from it too.
- A compact layout, the new default, as Safari's: one row with the space, back and forward, the tabs sharing the row at one width, new tab and the palette. The tab on screen shows where it is (or its title: Settings › The tab you are on shows; sign-in pages always show where); clicking a tab only goes to it, and clicking the one on screen turns it into the address field the way Safari's does: its words give way to the address, selected, in place, then the field widens over the other tabs, which stay where they are underneath, and Return, Escape or a click on the page takes it back into the tab along the same path. A pinned tab on screen stays an icon, so going to it moves nothing, unless it is on a sign-in page. Settings › Window › Tabs keeps the separate address bar instead.

A click in a pane gives it the keys and the bars, with a line in the space's colour along its top; drag the divider for more room on one side, or double-tap it for the middle. A tab's menu has Open in Split View, Separate Tabs and Swap Sides; the + button's menu, New Tab in Split View; ⌃⌥\\ splits the tab on screen with a new tab or separates it. Both pages stay live, closing one leaves the other, and a window narrower than two readable pages shows only the pane with the keys.

Import from Chrome, Firefox or Safari (Bookmarks.html, or the ZIP of Safari's Export Browsing Data) in Settings, on the empty grid, or with ⌃⌥⇧I.
- Sites' icons on tabs, pinned tabs and bookmarks, as the sites draw them, with no outline around them: the icon a page names, fetched once per site without cookies, and the ones a Chrome bookmarks file carries; a site without one shows its first letter.

- Safience, a WebKit browser for iPad, made for Figma and web apps used with a trackpad and keyboard. Pages are asked for in desktop mode as Mac Safari. A trackpad pinch and ⌘ with two fingers reach the page as ctrl+wheel at the pointer, as on a Mac, and two-finger scrolling is bridged when WebKit sends the page nothing; iPad's own page zoom, scrolling, swipe back and callouts are off. Sign-in pages always show the address, and Google's sign-in is never touched: no script, no cookie read.

### Changed

- The first launch makes the ad and tracker rule lists once. A second after the window is up it asks easylist.to for newer lists, waiting six seconds at most, and compiles whichever it then has; it used to compile the lists that shipped at launch and again when the newer ones came, each time with WebKit reading the rules on the main thread. Later launches check each list's version from its first lines rather than its megabytes, and a download is checked and written away from the main thread (`ContentBlocker.swift`).
- Less is drawn again while a page loads. The loading line's fraction and the phone bar's slide are published on their own (`LoadProgress`, `BarSlide`), the window's values only when they change, so progress no longer redraws the bars many times a second; a tab in the row is styled again only when something about it changed, and on iPhone, where the row doesn't show, it does no work at all; the star's look through the bookmarks is kept until the address or the bookmarks change.
- A trackpad's scroll over a bridged page sends one wheel call into the page at a time: the events that come while the page is still answering join into one, so a page slow to answer is no longer sent a queue of them; a pinch after a scroll waits its turn behind it, and a page whose process ended is owed no wait (`WheelLine`). The page script reads each ancestor's style once an event rather than once an axis, asks for the event's path only inside a shadow root, watches for a password field only while the tab shows, and polls the cursor's shape only while the pointer is over the page (`bridge.js`).
- Suggestions under the address get their candidates ready once for a stretch of typing rather than at every letter (`Autocomplete.Index`), ask Apple's model only for words it could do something with, and find out whether the model is there when it is first wanted rather than at launch. The palette ranks its rows once for each thing typed and gathers them once as it opens.
- Importing bookmarks reads, unzips and parses the file away from the main thread, and merging is one pass (a folder of a thousand links was a million comparisons); the icons a file carries are decoded and saved away from the main thread and the start page draws once for all of them.
- Three tabs' pictures at most are held in memory, let go first when memory runs short; the rest are read from disk as they are needed, the phone's slide to the tab beside reading its off the main thread.
- Close Other Tabs and Close All Tabs save the workspace once rather than once a tab. A page's address and title, which it says many times over, make a change to save and to tell the windows of only when one of them is new; iCloud is queued nothing when the open tabs haven't changed, and a send works out the bookmarks' places once.
- The first launch opens a new tab on the start page, not Figma: a site's sign-in page as the first thing on screen read as the app asking for an account (App Review took it for one).
- Split view's divider is a light grey band between the two pages, in a grey no page is, with the pages' corners along it rounded, so two white pages read as two; its handle grows taller and darker while it is dragged.
- Settings › Ads and trackers lists EasyList and EasyPrivacy, each with the day it was made, and the number of rules, as rows instead of a sentence under the switch.
- Settings no longer lists the sites and what each adapter does; Diagnostics still says which adapter a page has.
- Diagnostics' cursor line also says where the cursor's place comes from (a press, the hover recognizer, or off the page), how long ago the hover recognizer last spoke, and its state: a page cursor that stops moving can then be told from a page that stopped answering.
- The iPhone's desktop view and the new tab's start page run on under the bar as other pages do, so the bar's blur is over the page rather than a grey strip: the desktop view's cursor and view, and the start page's tiles, keep above the bar, and past the desktop's edge is the page's own colour.
- A desktop-view tab's picture in the tab overview is the part of the page that showed; a picture wider than a card fills its height and is cut at the side, never stretched.
- On iPhone, nothing is offered that does nothing there: Show or Hide Tab Bar, Split or Separate Tabs, Give Keys Back to the Page and Request Desktop or Mobile Site leave the palette, the palette shows no keyboard shortcuts (none work on iPhone), what the app says names the iPhone rather than the iPad, and the diagnostics panel sits above the bar.
- The tabs sheet's haptics are firmer: a medium tap where letting go opens or closes it, a rigid knock as it lands open, and a lighter one as it sinks back into the bar after a drag.
- On iPhone, Open in New Window, New Window and Close Window are gone from the space menu and the palette: an iPhone app has one window. Settings shows only what an iPhone uses: whether the address bar shows the page's address or its title, without the iPad's layout, tab bar, trackpad and shortcut settings.
- The menu of the tab on screen has what the phone's page menu (…) has: Share Page, Bookmark This Page or Remove Bookmark, Pin or Unpin Tab, and the command palette and Settings at the end, beside the tab's own items, in groups.
- New Tab, Close Tab, Reopen Closed Tab, New Window and Close Window are on ⌘T, ⌘W, ⇧⌘T, ⌘N and ⇧⌘W, and the next and previous tab on ⌃⇥ and ⌃⇧⇥, as in every browser and tabbed app, in place of their ⌃⌥ chords: these are the keys browsers keep from pages (Chrome reserves them), so no page counts on them. Every other shortcut stays on ⌃⌥.
- The iPad's menu bar has one Window menu: iPadOS's own, which shows whatever an app does, with Show or Hide Tab Bar, Diagnostics and Give Keys Back to the Page at its top and its items' ⌘ keys taken off. Settings… (⌃⌥,) moved to the Safience menu, above iPadOS Settings…, iPadOS's item that opens Safience's page in the Settings app.
- Only the tab on screen is in Liquid Glass; every other tab is flat, with a faint see-through shade under the pointer that takes on the bar's colour.
- Tapping or clicking the address selects all of it, so typing replaces it, as in Safari.
- Bars are 44 points tall, so every control answers in at least 40 by 40, and presses give a little (to 0.96).
- A link opened from another app opens in a new tab after the one on screen, instead of replacing that tab's page.
- The address bar shows on every page, so the address and the way back stay in view. Settings › Show the address bar can keep it to sign-in pages, as before.

### Fixed

- Other apps open from a site's own button. A page that went to another app's link by itself (an "Open in app" button whose script sets the address, `window.open`, a timer) was dropped without a word, and one asked for in a new window left an empty tab behind and opened nothing; only a link tapped directly opened its app. The page now asks first, as Safari does: "Open in Another App?", naming the site and the link's scheme, and Open hands the link over. A tapped link still opens at once, and a universal link tapped on another site is WebKit's to open, as before. A frame inside the page and a tab that isn't on screen are never let ask, one question shows at a time, and a second Cancel stands until the page changes, so a page that keeps trying asks twice at most (`AppLink.swift`). A window opened only to go to another app closes again, and when nothing on the device opens the link the app says so.
- Importing a bookmarks file that ends inside a tag (a download cut short) no longer stops the app: the reader ran past the end of the file (`BookmarkFile`).
- A window restored with a tab that has been closed since shows the tab its space is on, rather than "Nothing open" over a space that has tabs.
- The palette's rows for another device's tabs are told apart by their place, so two tabs on one address are two rows rather than one identity SwiftUI draws twice.
- Settings › App icon's pictures show on iPad: they were kept at 3x only, which an iPad, at 2x, does not load, so each is kept once for every scale.
- The tabs from other devices in the tab overview show an iPad's tabs under an iPad, not an iPhone.
- Settings on iPhone: the address bar's two choices are rows with a checkmark; the menu beside the long label wrapped onto a line of its own.
- Safience Sync for Chrome: a bookmark Chrome failed to make (an error mid-sync) is made on the next sync, rather than read as removed in Chrome and deleted from iCloud; deletions not yet made in Chrome are kept for it too, and a bookmark is removed with Chrome's call for a bookmark, a folder with the one for a folder.
- Safience Sync for Chrome: a pinned tab closed or unpinned in Chrome stays closed, instead of the next sync opening it again from iCloud's copy before the deletion went out.
- iCloud sync: a change made on this device and not yet sent survives a fetch of another device's version of the same record; it goes out on top of that version instead of being put back.
- iCloud sync: a device offline when sync first started joins when it next comes to the front, rather than at the next launch.
- `project.yml`, which makes the Xcode project, carries the build number and the alternate icons, so regenerating it no longer takes the build number back to 7.
- Tab in a page's field moves to the next field again. iPadOS 26's focus system keeps Tab from the page, so nothing moved; Tab now reaches the page first on every site, as it did only where an adapter or Settings asked, and the page script moves the focus when the page doesn't: through tab order, and from a field inside a shadow root to the next field after it rather than the page's first.
- Safience Sync for Chrome: a bookmark renamed, moved or removed in Chrome stays that way. The sync the edit set off wrote iCloud's older copy back over it (the old title or place, or the bookmark again) before sending it; Chrome now takes iCloud's bookmarks only when iCloud's changed, and a bookmark Chrome no longer has is deleted from iCloud by the next push rather than made again.
- The row of tabs lays itself out again when the first ordinary tab is pinned or the last pinned tab unpinned: the order stayed the same, so the tab kept its old width until something else moved.
- A trackpad's pointer over a round or capsule button on the bars (new tab, the palette, back, forward, the space, close, the star, reload) lit up a square; it now takes the shape drawn.
- A right-click (a trackpad's secondary click) on a tab opened no menu: the tab's tap answered the click first. The row of tabs is UIKit now (TabStrip.swift), where a tab's tap answers the primary button alone and the click goes on to the menu; the pointer's highlight takes the tab's shape and follows the row as it scrolls, and the close button, the star and reload are controls of their own inside the tab.
- Return in the command palette went to the first row, not the one chosen with the arrows: the field writes its text back as Return sends it, which the palette took for new typing.
- Typing an address on an iPhone stopped at once: select all, sent to whatever had the keys, reached the page, which took them back. The field now selects its own text.
- The palette and the address field left out the last letter typed until the next key: the palette offered "Open github.co" for github.com, and Return opened github.co.
