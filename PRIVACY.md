# Privacy Policy

**eGlu** for iPhone, iPad and Mac · Effective October 23, 2026

eGlu is a web browser made by Taehee Jung. This policy says what the app does with your information. In short: **eGlu collects nothing.** It has no account, no server, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. What it keeps stays on your device, or in your own iCloud account if you turn on iCloud sync.

## What eGlu collects

Nothing. The developer receives no data from the app: not what you browse, not what you search, not how you use the app. There is no sign-up, and the app contains no analytics, advertising or tracking code from anyone.

## What leaves your device

Only what using a browser needs:

- **The sites you visit.** Pages load from the sites you open, as in any browser. Those sites see your visits and may collect information under their own privacy policies.
- **Your searches.** Words you type that aren't an address go to the search engine you choose in Settings (Google unless you change it), under that engine's privacy policy.
- **Search suggestions.** With Settings › Search › Search engine suggestions on (it is unless you turn it off), what you type in the address bar goes to that search engine as you type, so it can suggest searches, if the engine offers them (Google, DuckDuckGo and Bing do). Complete addresses are excluded once recognized, but a partial address can be sent before it is recognized. The requests carry no cookies or sign-ins. Turn search engine suggestions off to keep address-bar typing on your device. The suggestions from your tabs, bookmarks and the app's commands are made on your device and go nowhere.
- **Sites' icons.** The app downloads the icon a page you visited names, without sending your cookies or sign-ins.
- **Ad-blocking lists.** With ad blocking on (Settings › Ads and trackers, on unless you turn it off), the app downloads the filter lists that are turned on in Settings › Ads and trackers › Filter lists, each from its authors' server (easylist.to, easylist-downloads.adblockplus.org, secure.fanboy.co.nz or raw.githubusercontent.com), at most once every four days, and only if it changed. A request carries no cookies and nothing about you or what you browse; like any server, these see your device's IP address. The blocking itself happens on your device.
- **A page you hand off**, if you turn on Handoff (Settings › Handoff, off unless you turn it on). When you choose Handoff for a page and then pick an app in the share sheet, that app gets a file with the page's text (first, anything you had selected on the page), the page's address and the time the page was read, and a line for your question. The text is the page as it showed to you: if you are signed in to a site, what it shows you alone is part of it. The address goes without the tags that say how you came to the page (`utm_source`, `fbclid` and the like), and the time is by Greenwich's clock, so it does not say which time zone you are in. It goes to the app you pick and nowhere else, under that app's privacy policy, and nothing goes until you pick one. For a video on YouTube the file also has the video's transcript, which the app reads from YouTube's own page. On an iPhone, where YouTube's page shows no transcript, the app loads the video's page at youtube.com a second time, out of sight, as you are signed in or not on that site, reads the transcript there and closes it: YouTube sees that as another visit to the video's page. Nothing is played, and the app asks YouTube for nothing else.
- **Your spaces, bookmarks and pinned tabs, if you turn on iCloud sync** (Settings › iCloud, off unless you turn it on). Each space's name, colour, icon, bookmarks and pinned tabs are kept in your own iCloud account by Apple (CloudKit's private database), under Apple's privacy policy, so they show on your other iPhones, iPads and Macs. The developer can't read them. Your open tabs stay on each device and are not synced, and neither are your sign-ins, cookies and what sites store.
- **Your history, if you turn on Sync history in Settings › iCloud** (off unless you turn it on, and only with iCloud sync on). The pages you went to in each space, when, their titles and descriptions, and the searches you typed in the address bar, are kept in your own iCloud account (the handoffs in History are not: they stay on the device that made them) by Apple in CloudKit's encrypted fields: encrypted in transit and on Apple's servers, and end to end with Advanced Data Protection on. They show in History and the address bar on your other iPhones, iPads and Macs. The developer can't read them. Clearing history on one device clears it on all of them: the clearing itself is kept in iCloud for at most 400 days, so a device that was offline clears it too, and a page cleared is named in it only by a one-way digest, never by its address. Turning Sync history off deletes that device's history from iCloud; what your other devices already have stays on them. iCloud keeps no history older than 400 days.

## On a Mac: Chrome extensions

eGlu for Mac runs Chrome extensions you add. An extension is someone else's software: what it does with what it can read is under its developer's privacy policy, not this one. On the sites you allow it, an extension can read and change the page as the page's own scripts can, and send what it reads elsewhere. eGlu limits what any extension may reach: none gets Chrome's cookies API, none runs on Google's sign-in (accounts.google.com), and each reads only the sites you allowed it when you added it, or only the page you click it on.

What the app keeps on your Mac for them, sent to no one:

| What | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Each extension's files, as they came and as prepared to run | To run it |
| What each extension stores, kept apart for each space it is on in | Its settings and sign-ins, as it keeps them. Removing it from a space deletes them there |
| What an extension ran into: which of its features failed, where in it, how often, when it first and last happened, and the versions of the extension, eGlu and macOS. Never an error's words, a page or an address | To say when something doesn't work, and for a report if you choose to send one. At most 200 an extension, for 30 days, deleted with the extension |
| The results of trying an extension's pages when it was added, in a temporary space that keeps no data | So they aren't tried again until something changes |
| Messages between an extension and a desktop app on your Mac that goes with it (Bitwarden's, 1Password's), through that app's helper | So the extension can work with the app. They stay between the two on your Mac; what the app does next is under its own policy. If the extension didn't ask for this when you added it, eGlu asks you first |

What leaves your Mac for them:

- **The extensions you add, and their updates**, downloaded from Google's Chrome Web Store, which sees each download as any store does.
- **A check for updates about once a day** (Settings › Extensions, on unless you turn it off; again within the hour if it fails; Check Now checks at once): the ids and versions of your Chrome Web Store extensions go to Google's update server, as Chrome's check does, so it can say which have a newer version.
- **What an extension sends itself.** Its pages and worker reach the servers it uses, under its developer's policy. Its worker's WebSocket connections go through eGlu, to the server the extension names, with no cookies but with whatever the extension sends. When an extension is added, its pages are tried once in a temporary space, and can reach those servers then too.
- **The list of your Chrome Web Store extensions**, if iCloud sync is on: each one's store id, name and site access, and the spaces it is on in, kept in your own iCloud account in encrypted fields (end to end if you turn on Advanced Data Protection), so your other Macs can offer them. The developer can't read it. Removing an extension on one Mac removes it, with what it kept, on your other Macs. Extensions added from a file or a folder are left out.
- **A report, only if you send it.** Report a Problem… shows everything in it; you copy or save it and send it where you like. eGlu sends nothing.

## Permissions

The app asks the system for these only when they are needed, and you can turn each off in the Settings app at any time:

- **Camera and microphone**, when a site you use asks for them, for a video call for example. They go to that site, never to the developer.
- **Files**, only for a file you choose: a bookmarks file to import, where to save a download, or on a Mac an extension to add. On a Mac, eGlu runs outside the App Sandbox, so that extensions can reach the desktop apps that go with them.

Passwords, passkeys and verification codes are handled by Apple's Passwords app and AutoFill. eGlu does not store them.

On a Mac, where you turn on Settings › General › Apple Passwords under sign-in boxes (only in a copy of eGlu Apple has let use passkeys), eGlu asks Apple's own Passwords helper on your Mac for the account names it keeps for the site you are signing in to, and for one password once you pick its account, to fill it into that page. The pairing code you type and the key it makes stay in memory until eGlu quits; no account or password is written down or sent anywhere else. So that it offers itself at most once on a site, the sites where it did are remembered on your Mac.

## Crash reports

If you choose to share analytics with app developers (Settings › Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements), Apple may share crash reports with the developer to help fix problems. You can turn this off at any time.

## Deleting your data

- Remove a space to delete its tabs, bookmarks and website data, its sign-ins included.
- Close a tab to delete its page and its picture. The addresses of the last 25 closed tabs are kept, so you can reopen them.
- Clear History (in History, or Settings › History) to delete your history from the last hour, today, today and yesterday, or all of it. With Sync history on in Settings › iCloud, it is cleared on your other devices and in iCloud too.
- Delete the app to delete everything it kept on an iPhone or iPad. On a Mac, deleting the app leaves its folders: delete eGlu's folders in your Library's Application Support, Caches and WebKit folders, and its preferences, too. Spaces, bookmarks and pinned tabs you remove while iCloud sync is on are removed from iCloud too: each is kept there, marked removed, for 30 days, so your other devices remove it as well, and then deleted.
- On a Mac, remove an extension from eGlu to delete its files, what it kept in every space, and its records.
