Tide observer

Harbour Board · Dover · Full time · 31,000 to 36,000 GBP a year

About the role

The tide observer keeps the record the harbour runs on. Twice a day, and more often in a gale, you read the gauges on the outer wall, check them against the predicted heights, and post what the sea is really doing for the pilots, the ferries and the lifeboat crew. When the readings and the tables part company, you are the first to know and the one who says so.

What you will do

  • Read and log the tide gauges at high and low water, by hand and from the automatic station.
  • Post heights and warnings to the harbour's channel and the pilots' board.
  • Keep the gauges clean, level and calibrated, with the engineers.
  • Send the month's record to the national tide service.
  • Stand watch one weekend in four, and during storm surges.

What we ask for

  • A careful hand with numbers, and the habit of writing down what you see.
  • Two years of work outdoors in any weather, at sea or on the shore.
  • A full driving licence: the gauges are spread along four miles of coast.
  • Swimming, and a sea survival course, or the will to take one in your first month.

What we offer

  • 31,000 to 36,000 GBP a year, with an allowance for storm watches.
  • 28 days of leave, and the harbour's own pension.
  • Training with the national tide service in your first year.

How to apply

Send a short letter and your record of work by 15 November. We read every letter ourselves and answer each one within two weeks; the interviews are held on the harbour wall, in whatever weather the day brings.
